# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Single and solo moms currently *in* the empty nest — living it day to day, not people who already moved through it and came out the other side. This is not "empty nesters" as a general demographic, and it is not framed as a transition with an endpoint to reach. It's an ongoing life stage the audience is inside of right now.

## Product Purpose

My Nest Chapter (workbooks, freebies, and interactive HTML tools like the Garage Sale Planner and Cooking for One) gives solo moms practical support for daily life in the empty nest — tools built for repeat, ongoing use during a stage that doesn't resolve on a timeline, not one-time "get through this and move on" resources.

## Positioning

Cece, the brand's voice, is not a coach or expert who processed this stage and is now teaching it from the other side. She has lived experience *and* is living it every day, currently, alongside her audience. The brand's mechanism is peer solidarity in a shared present experience — mom-to-mom, first person "I," present tense for her current life and past tense for specific stories — not retrospective wisdom from someone who has "arrived." This is what a coaching-framework or therapy-speak competitor could not truthfully copy.

## Operating Context

Custom PHP/HTML site on Hostinger. Stripe live for payments. Amazon KDP for the Workbook 1 paperback. Member dashboard with gated freebies (public Freebies page requires email capture; logged-in members get everything gate-free) and a monthly exclusive-content drop cadence. Canva used for print/graphic asset creation (Brand Kit `kAHE-_Dmcm0`).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Interactive HTML tools are the repeat-use product format (vs. Workbook for long-form guided work, Companion for shorter topics, PDF for one-time concepts).
- New widget builds follow a mandatory gated process (Gate 1 define → Gate 2 design plan → Gate 3 QA) documented in `CLAUDE.md`; DESIGN.md is the canonical authority for any visual/color/font decision.
- One-repo discipline: no duplicate project docs live outside this repository.
- Undecided/open: Someday List Companion final price ($7.99 placeholder); Goal + Habit Tracker visual rebuild is back-burner, no brief yet.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **My Nest Chapter** — always three words, each capitalized, never abbreviated (never "MNC" in user-facing copy).
- Tagline: **Solo mom. Empty nest. Now what?**
- Voice: mom-to-mom, not coach-to-client. First person "I" always — never "we," never third person, never "the brand." Cece speaks for herself only, never on behalf of "solo moms" collectively.
- Audience framing (explicit, corrected 2026-07-16 in DESIGN.md): never characterize the reader as stressed, overwhelmed, low-tech, or fragile. She's capable and tech-comfortable; design and copy start from respect and competence, not hand-holding.
- Possibility language only — outcome promises ("this will help you," "you'll feel") are banned brand-wide in favor of process language ("this helped me," "you might notice").

## Product Principles

- Peer solidarity from someone currently living the same stage, not expertise handed down from someone who's "arrived" — this governs voice, not just visual tone.
- Tools serve an ongoing, repeat-use life stage, not a transition with a finish line — favor durable, reusable formats over one-and-done resources.
- Start every design and copy decision from the audience's competence and self-respect; fragility is never the justification for a UX or tone choice.
- One committed brand story at a time — avoid mixing extra visual or voice elements just because they're available.
- Never promise outcomes; describe process and lived experience instead.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Minimum 44×44px touch targets on interactive elements (mobile-first, most widget use happens on a phone). ARIA labels and roles required on interactive widget elements.
